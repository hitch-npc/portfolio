Where it started

The system looked coherent
as long as the screens were looked at one at a time.

Side by side lived thirteen ways to set the same grey caption, three principles for marking an active chip, and cobalt on ten-pixel text. None of those choices was wrong when it was made — what was wrong is that each one was made from scratch. That is drift: the rule is written nowhere, so it gets invented again every time.

A UX audit went through ten blocks of the user journey — from the first screen to the email after purchase. Every hypothesis got a success metric and a RICE score, so the argument was about order of work, not taste. What entered the design system is what won on points, not what was more fun to draw.

Every fix carries a measurement,
not “this looks nicer”.

Decisions

A decision is a line in a file,
not somebody’s memory.

Every rule has a reason, a date and a condition for revision. A decision can change — but only together with its record, in the same commit. A change without a record counts as drift and gets rolled back. A decision can be challenged with a measurement or a screen where the rule breaks; “I like it better this way” is accepted too — from the owner of the system, and it gets written down as well.

Decision №1 · 12.09.2026
Primary buttons are always cobalt.
Reason
One accent per system. The green button gave 2.3 : 1 against a 4.5 : 1 requirement.
Where it applies
“Buy”, “Go to checkout”, every primary CTA.
To revise it
A background + text pair with contrast ≥ 4.5 : 1 in both themes.
Decision №7 · 11.09.2026
Cormorant only from 22px; below that, Forum 400.
Reason
The thin strokes of Cormorant shimmer at small sizes — it shows on listing cards.
Where it applies
Event titles, card subtitles, category tiles.
To revise it
A face with more stroke contrast at 15–20px.
Decision №13 · 12.09.2026
Barcode — dark bars on a light plate, never inverted.
Reason
A scanner needs a light background; not every model reads an inverted code.
Where it applies
The e-ticket in both themes, mobile and desktop.
To revise it
Proof that the target scanners read inversion.
Decision №20 · 15.09.2026
One footer for the whole site, darker than the page.
Reason
Two different footers on neighbouring pages read as two different sites.
Where it applies
The redesign, the partner page, every public build.
To revise it
The owner’s decision — only the owner revises it.

A separate list holds deliberate exceptions: caps on the first screen of the partner page, the show title in caps on tickets, font-weight: 300 on the seat-map zoom button. An exception written down with a reason is not a breach of the system but a part of it.
